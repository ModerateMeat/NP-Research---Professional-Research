# Writing Standards

## Default voice

Professional nurse-practitioner writing should be clinically precise, readable, natural, evidence-aware, and calibrated to the intended audience. Avoid inflated certainty, unnecessary jargon, and generic AI-sounding filler.

## Keep layers separate

When useful, maintain separate artifacts for:

- source notes;
- evidence synthesis;
- working draft;
- fact/citation check;
- final/publication version.

This prevents stylistic editing from silently changing evidence or certainty.

## Claims

- Evidence-dependent factual claims should be traceable to inspected sources.
- Distinguish established knowledge from interpretation, hypothesis, or opinion.
- Do not strengthen a source's conclusion during paraphrase.
- Do not introduce clinical recommendations solely because they make the prose sound more complete.

## Originality

Synthesize in original language. Avoid patchwriting or closely tracking source sentence structure. Use direct quotations sparingly and only when exact wording matters.

## Audience

Match structure and terminology to the deliverable: professional social post, patient education, academic synthesis, clinical discussion, presentation text, or publication draft. The same evidence may require different explanation without changing the underlying meaning.

## Final review

Before publication/submission, check:

- factual and citation accuracy;
- clinical meaning and certainty;
- privacy/de-identification;
- audience fit;
- grammar/readability;
- unsupported additions introduced during editing.
