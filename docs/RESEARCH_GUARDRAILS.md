# Research and Clinical Guardrails

## Evidence integrity

For clinical, scientific, or professional claims:

- distinguish primary studies, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, clinical guidelines, regulatory documents, consensus statements, and secondary commentary;
- prefer authoritative and current sources when recency matters;
- inspect a source before citing it;
- never fabricate DOI, PMID, URL, quotation, page number, sample size, effect size, confidence interval, p-value, or bibliographic detail;
- separate what a source reports from inference or opinion;
- record conflicting evidence and important limitations;
- verify clinically important numeric claims against the underlying source before publication;
- do not treat manufacturer/vendor marketing as equivalent to independent evidence.

Record the search/review date for time-sensitive topics.

## Clinical claims

- Do not present preliminary, preclinical, exploratory, or low-certainty findings as established clinical practice.
- Clearly distinguish FDA-approved indications from off-label, investigational, compounded, or unapproved uses when relevant.
- For dosing/treatment content, identify source and context rather than presenting unsourced regimens as universal practice.
- Include material contraindications, monitoring considerations, population limits, and safety signals when they change interpretation.
- Avoid diagnostic or treatment claims that extend beyond the reviewed evidence.
- Preserve jurisdictional and scope-of-practice context when discussing nurse-practitioner laws or practice authority.

## Privacy

- Do not commit PHI or patient-identifiable information.
- Remove direct and indirect identifiers from clinical examples.
- Use the minimum clinical detail needed for the research/writing purpose.
- Do not move sensitive source content from Drive or other systems into GitHub merely for convenience.

## Human review

AI-assisted synthesis and drafting require human clinical judgment before external publication or use in patient-care decisions. A polished draft is not the same as a verified evidence review.
