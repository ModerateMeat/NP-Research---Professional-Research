# Source and Citation Policy

## Preferred evidence order

Choose the source type that best answers the question rather than mechanically following a hierarchy. In general, prioritize:

1. Primary research for what a study actually found.
2. Systematic reviews/meta-analyses for broader evidence synthesis.
3. Current clinical guidelines and consensus statements for practice recommendations.
4. Regulators and official agencies for approvals, warnings, labeling, laws, and public-health data.
5. Authoritative professional organizations and academic references for context.
6. High-quality secondary sources for orientation, not as a substitute for primary verification when precision matters.

## Connected sources

When relevant material exists in Google Drive, search/read it through the Drive connector first. Preserve the original. Repository notes should point to or identify the source without unnecessarily duplicating copyrighted or sensitive content.

## Source record

A topic's `sources.md` should capture enough information to re-find and verify important sources. Recommended fields:

```markdown
- Citation:
- Source type:
- DOI/PMID/URL or connected-file identifier:
- Publication/update date:
- Date inspected:
- Relevant question/claim:
- Key limitations:
- Verification status:
```

## Citation verification

Before a source-dependent deliverable is considered final:

- confirm the source exists;
- confirm author/title/journal/year or issuing body;
- confirm the cited source actually supports the claim;
- confirm quoted language is exact and minimal;
- confirm important numbers from the underlying source;
- flag retractions, corrections, major conflicts, or outdated guidance when discovered.

Never create a plausible-looking citation to fill a gap. Mark the gap instead.
