## Cross-source synthesis rules

### 1. Reference texts are navigation aids, not automatic final authority

These sources are most valuable for generating search terms, identifying relevant clinical domains, understanding conceptual frameworks, and spotting questions that need stronger evidence. A later research slice should replace or supplement textbook-level orientation with the best current evidence for the actual claim.

### 2. Regulatory and legal claims use official sources

For medication approval/safety claims, prioritize current FDA materials and official labeling. For Texas NP scope, prescriptive authority, delegation/collaboration requirements, or licensure rules, use current Texas Board of Nursing materials and applicable statutes/rules. Certification claims should use current credentialing-body sources.

### 3. Treatment claims require topic-specific evidence

A source in this foundational map does not by itself justify statements such as:

- a therapy is first-line;
- one medication is superior to another;
- a dose is standard or optimal;
- a treatment is safe for a special population;
- a screening instrument should be universally used;
- a mechanism explains a clinical outcome.

Those statements require a new bounded research question and targeted evidence review.

### 4. Special populations increase the verification burden

Pediatric/adolescent, geriatric, pregnancy/lactation, substance-use, hepatic/renal impairment, cardiovascular-risk, and other special-population claims should be verified with population-specific evidence and current guidance.

### 5. Provenance affects handling, not scientific truth—but it still matters

A scientifically valid published work does not become scientifically false because a connected copy has uncertain acquisition provenance. However, uncertain or non-publisher provenance affects how the file may be handled: do not redistribute it, do not treat the connected copy as proof of authorized access, and prefer lawful publisher/library/official access when creating publication-facing citations or sharing source materials.
