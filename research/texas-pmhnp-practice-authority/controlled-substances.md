# Texas PMHNP Controlled-Substance Prescribing

**Slice:** 4 of Issue #7  
**Review date:** September 3, 2026  
**Status:** current working synthesis based on Texas BON, Texas statutes, Texas State Board of Pharmacy, and DEA sources

## Bottom line

A Texas PMHNP does **not** gain controlled-substance authority merely by holding an APRN license or a DEA registration.

The authority stack is:

**PMHNP role/population-focus scope → Texas APRN prescriptive authority → physician-delegated prescriptive authority → valid PAA/facility-based authorization → Texas schedule-specific limits → DEA registration → PMP/e-prescribing/other state and federal requirements.**

If any layer is missing, a DEA number does not independently create authority to prescribe the controlled substance.

## 1. Texas and DEA authority must align

Texas BON guidance requires the APRN to hold full Texas APRN licensure and the applicable Texas prescriptive authority before prescribing controlled substances and before obtaining/using the corresponding DEA authority.

DEA likewise explains that a practitioner or mid-level practitioner must possess authority under the law of the state where the practitioner engages in professional practice. State law determines which schedules and activities the practitioner may lawfully handle.

### Practical rule

**DEA registration follows state authority; it does not expand it.**

A Texas PMHNP must use the PMHNP's own DEA registration when prescribing controlled substances. The PMHNP may not prescribe under the delegating physician's DEA registration.

Texas no longer requires a separate Texas Department of Public Safety controlled-substance registration.

## 2. Schedule III-V authority

Texas BON guidance states that physicians may delegate authority to APRNs to prescribe controlled substances in **Schedules III, IV, and V** in practice settings when all Texas delegation, prescriptive-authority, and federal requirements are satisfied.

The PAA or applicable facility-based authorization must permit the relevant controlled-substance category.

### 90-day supply/consultation rule

Board Rule 222.8 establishes a specific APRN limitation for Schedule III-V controlled-substance prescribing:

- the prescription, including refills, may not exceed a **90-day supply**;
- if continued therapy requires another prescription/refill after the initial 90 days, the APRN must consult with the delegating physician;
- the consultation must be documented in the patient's medical record; and
- physician consultation is required again every 90 days as long as the APRN continues prescribing the controlled substance under this framework.

Current BON guidance clarifies that this 90-day clock concerns the patient's controlled-substance therapy, not merely whether the APRN personally wrote the first prescription. A patient already taking the medication for at least 90 days may therefore require physician consultation before the APRN continues it.

### Patient younger than two years

For a child younger than two years, an APRN may not prescribe a Schedule III-V controlled substance before consultation with the delegating physician and documentation of that consultation in the patient's chart.

## 3. Schedule II authority is much narrower

Texas law places substantially tighter limits on physician delegation of Schedule II prescribing to APRNs.

Current BON Rule 222.8 and BON guidance identify Schedule II authority only in the following circumstances:

### Hospital facility-based practice

The APRN is practicing in a qualifying hospital facility-based practice under the applicable hospital policies and the Schedule II prescription/order is part of care for a patient who:

- has been admitted to the hospital with an intended length of stay of **24 hours or greater**; or
- is receiving services in the hospital's emergency department.

### Qualified hospice care

The Schedule II substance is part of the plan of care for a person who:

- has executed the required written certification of terminal illness;
- has elected hospice care; and
- is receiving hospice treatment from a qualified hospice provider.

### No general outpatient Schedule II delegation

Outside the statutory circumstances above, a physician may not simply add Schedule II authority to an outpatient PMHNP PAA.

A freestanding emergency department affiliated with a hospital does not qualify as the hospital facility-based location for Schedule II delegation under current BON guidance.

## 4. Schedule II prescriptions at hospital discharge

Current BON FAQ guidance states that an APRN's Schedule II prescription issued as part of the hospital discharge process must be filled at the **hospital's facility-based pharmacy** for the prescription to remain within the permitted facility-based authority.

If the prescription is intended to be filled outside that qualifying hospital facility-based setting, the APRN's delegated Schedule II authority does not extend to that prescription; a physician would need to issue the prescription if clinically appropriate and legally authorized.

This requirement is operationally important for discharge planning and patient education.

## 5. The APRN's own DEA registration is required

A Texas PMHNP who will prescribe controlled substances must obtain and maintain an appropriate DEA registration consistent with Texas authority.

DEA states that controlled-substance privileges are constrained by the state authority on which the DEA registration is based. DEA also requires separate registration for each principal place of business or professional practice when federal registration rules require it.

For a Texas application, DEA instructions require valid state licensure/authority and, for mid-level practitioners, applicable supervisory/delegation documentation when required by state law.

### Prescription identifiers

Texas BON guidance states that controlled-substance prescription orders include the applicable DEA identifiers required by Texas law/rule, including the APRN's DEA number and the delegating physician's DEA information where required.

The exact prescription-field requirements should be checked against current Rule 222.4 and Texas Occupations Code §157.056 when implementing an EHR template.

## 6. Texas Prescription Monitoring Program (PMP)

The Texas PMP tracks Schedule II-V controlled substances dispensed by Texas pharmacies and controlled substances dispensed to Texas residents from pharmacies in other states.

### Mandatory PMP lookup

Texas Health and Safety Code §481.0764 and current Texas State Board of Pharmacy/BON guidance require prescribers, other than veterinarians, to access the patient's PMP history **before prescribing**:

- opioids;
- benzodiazepines;
- barbiturates; or
- carisoprodol.

The PMP may also be reviewed before prescribing other controlled substances even when a mandatory lookup is not triggered.

### Statutory exceptions

Texas Health and Safety Code §481.0765 provides an exception to the mandatory lookup when:

- the patient has been diagnosed with cancer;
- the patient has been diagnosed with sickle cell disease; or
- the patient is receiving hospice care;

and the prescriber clearly documents the applicable exception in the prescription record.

The statute also recognizes a good-faith inability-to-access exception for circumstances outside the prescriber's control, subject to the required documentation.

### BON documentation expectation

BON Rule 228.2 requires documentation in the patient's medical record that the PMP was reviewed and the rationale for prescribing the controlled substance.

An APRN may have an authorized employee/agent perform the PMP query where permitted, but current BON guidance states that the APRN remains responsible for reviewing the information and documenting the review and prescribing rationale.

### Hospital medication order vs discharge prescription

BON distinguishes a **medication order** for a drug administered while the patient is receiving care in a hospital/ambulatory surgical center from a **prescription drug order** dispensed by a pharmacy.

Current BON guidance states that the mandatory PMP check is not required merely to write the inpatient medication order, but a qualifying controlled-substance prescription intended to be filled upon discharge triggers the applicable PMP review/documentation requirements.

## 7. Electronic prescribing of controlled substances

Beginning January 1, 2021, Texas requires controlled-substance prescriptions to be issued electronically unless a statutory exception in Health and Safety Code §481.0755 applies or the prescriber has an applicable waiver under §481.0756.

Statutory exceptions include defined circumstances such as temporary technology failure, certain out-of-state pharmacy situations, circumstances involving unsupported data elements, specified research/non-patient-specific prescriptions, an approved waiver, or situations in which an electronic prescription would create an impractical delay that could adversely affect the patient.

### APRN waiver

BON may grant an individual APRN an electronic-prescribing waiver under the statutory/rule framework for circumstances such as:

- economic hardship;
- technological limitations outside the prescriber's reasonable control; or
- other demonstrated exceptional circumstances.

A waiver granted to a delegating physician does **not** automatically extend to the APRN. Each prescriber who requires a waiver must qualify for their own waiver.

## 8. Separate Texas acute-pain opioid limit

Do not confuse the APRN 90-day controlled-substance consultation rule with Texas's separate statewide opioid rule for **acute pain**.

Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 481 provides that, for treatment of acute pain, a practitioner generally may not:

- issue an opioid prescription exceeding a **10-day supply**; or
- provide a refill.

The statute contains defined exclusions/exceptions, including treatment contexts such as chronic pain, cancer care, hospice/end-of-life care, and palliative care, and it contains a specific provision concerning FDA-approved medication for substance-addiction treatment.

This 10-day acute-pain rule is a separate restriction that may produce a shorter lawful duration than the APRN's general 90-day Schedule III-V ceiling.

## 9. PMHNP examples: do not reason from the drug alone

For a Texas PMHNP, a medication being clinically common in psychiatric practice does not answer the legal-prescribing question.

### Example: benzodiazepine

A benzodiazepine is generally Schedule IV. Before prescribing, verify:

- PMHNP scope and competence;
- Texas prescriptive authority;
- physician-delegated Schedule IV authority in the PAA/protocol;
- DEA registration;
- 90-day/consultation requirements;
- mandatory PMP lookup and documentation;
- electronic-prescribing requirements; and
- employer policy/credentialing.

### Example: stimulant

Many stimulant medications used for ADHD are Schedule II. The fact that ADHD treatment is within psychiatric practice does **not** create outpatient Schedule II prescribing authority for a Texas PMHNP. Texas's Schedule II delegation limitations must be applied separately.

### Example: buprenorphine

Do not infer the applicable authority from older "X-waiver" concepts or from the medication's use in substance-use treatment. Verify the medication's current federal scheduling/DEA requirements, Texas delegation authority, PMHNP scope/competence, and any current federal treatment-specific rules at the time of prescribing.

## 10. Controlled-substance onboarding checklist

Before a Texas PMHNP prescribes any controlled substance, verify:

- [ ] The medication is within PMHNP role/population-focus scope and the individual's competence.
- [ ] Texas APRN license is active.
- [ ] Texas prescriptive authority/prescription authorization is active.
- [ ] General physician-delegation protocol is current.
- [ ] PAA or qualifying facility-based authorization permits the relevant controlled-substance schedule/category.
- [ ] TMB prescriptive-delegation registration is current.
- [ ] The PMHNP has their own active DEA registration appropriate to the Texas practice.
- [ ] Schedule II authority, if relevant, fits one of the narrow Texas statutory settings/circumstances.
- [ ] Schedule III-V prescriptions comply with the 90-day/physician-consultation framework.
- [ ] PMP lookup requirements and exceptions have been evaluated and documented.
- [ ] Electronic controlled-substance prescribing requirements are satisfied or a valid exception/individual waiver applies.
- [ ] Any separate opioid limits, pain-management rules, or patient-age consultation requirements apply.
- [ ] Employer credentialing, pharmacy workflow, and EHR prescription fields support compliant prescribing.
