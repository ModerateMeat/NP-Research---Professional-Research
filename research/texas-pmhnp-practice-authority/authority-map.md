## 1. Scope is a layered determination

The working model for every future question is:

**Licensed role/population focus → professional scope → Texas law/rules → individual education/competence → delegation when required → employer credentialing/privileging → patient/setting circumstances.**

No single layer should be used to bypass another.

### Example

A PMHNP may be professionally educated to diagnose and manage psychiatric disorders, but that does not answer all of the following:

- whether a specific procedure was included in the APRN's formal preparation or later competency development;
- whether Texas law/rules require physician delegation for the medical aspect involved;
- whether prescriptive authority has been granted for a medication/device;
- whether the employer has credentialed/privileged the PMHNP for that service;
- whether a controlled-substance or facility-specific limitation applies.

## 2. Delegation does not create a new APRN specialty

The Texas BON scope framework makes role and population-focus education foundational. Physician delegation is an additional authority mechanism for delegated medical acts; it is not a mechanism for converting a PMHNP into another APRN role/population focus.

This distinction should be preserved in all future repo writing.

## 3. Prescriptive authority is a separate analysis

Do not infer prescribing authority merely because a medication is clinically within psychiatric practice.

For a prescribing question, verify at least:

1. active Texas APRN licensure in the relevant role/population focus;
2. Texas prescriptive authority/prescription authorization status;
3. the applicable physician delegation mechanism;
4. whether the drug/device category is authorized by that agreement/protocol;
5. whether additional controlled-substance, DEA, PMP, e-prescribing, telemedicine, or setting limits apply;
6. whether the employer permits the prescribing activity.

## 4. PAA and facility-based pathways must not be conflated

For this project, always identify the practice setting before describing the delegation document.

- **Non-facility-based practice:** analyze the written PAA pathway.
- **Qualifying facility-based practice:** analyze whether prescribing operates through a PAA or qualifying facility-based protocols/other written authorization.

A clinic merely owned by or associated with a hospital is not automatically treated as the hospital facility-based practice for every statutory purpose; exact setting definitions belong in Slice 3/4.

## 5. Controlled substances require their own slice

Do not summarize Texas controlled-substance authority as "APRNs can prescribe controlled substances." The schedule, setting, patient circumstances, delegation, DEA status, consultation requirements, PMP/e-prescribing rules, and telemedicine rules can materially change the answer.

The next controlled-substance slice must separately analyze:

- Schedule II;
- Schedule III-V;
- initial vs continuing/refill prescribing and physician consultation;
- DEA registration;
- Texas PMP duties;
- controlled-substance electronic prescribing;
- telemedicine restrictions;
- hospital/facility and hospice exceptions.

## 6. Current working conclusions suitable for internal orientation

As of the September 3, 2026 review:

1. A Texas PMHNP's professional/legal scope is rooted in PMHNP education, BON-licensed role/population focus, professional scope standards, individual competence, and Texas law/rules.
2. Physician delegation does not expand the APRN's licensed role/population-focus scope.
3. Delegated medical aspects of care require the appropriate physician-delegation mechanism.
4. Prescribing is not inherent merely in APRN title; the Texas prescriptive-authority and delegation requirements must be satisfied.
5. PAA and facility-based protocol pathways differ by practice setting.
6. Controlled-substance authority must be analyzed separately from non-controlled prescribing.
7. Schedule II authority is materially narrower than Schedule III-V authority in Texas.
8. Employer credentialing and privileges may narrow what the individual PMHNP actually performs even when an act is within the broader professional/legal scope.
