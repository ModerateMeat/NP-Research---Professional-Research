# Research Question: Texas PMHNP Practice Authority & Professional Scope

**Review date:** September 3, 2026  
**Parent issue:** #7

## Primary question

As of September 3, 2026, what legal, regulatory, licensure, delegation, prescriptive-authority, controlled-substance, and role/population-focus boundaries govern a Texas-licensed psychiatric-mental health nurse practitioner (PMHNP)?

## Why this question matters

Texas PMHNP practice authority is distributed across nursing law/rules, physician-delegation law, prescribing rules, controlled-substance requirements, professional scope standards, and employer credentialing. A durable authority map reduces the risk of relying on oversimplified statements such as "NPs can" or "NPs cannot" without identifying the controlling source and setting.

## Structured frame

- **Population / professional role:** Texas-licensed APRNs practicing in the psychiatric-mental health nurse practitioner role/population focus.
- **Intervention / exposure:** Clinical practice, medical diagnosis/management, delegated medical acts, prescribing, controlled-substance prescribing, and telemedicine.
- **Comparator:** Not applicable; this is a legal/regulatory scope question.
- **Outcomes:** Clear identification of what authority governs each practice question and what additional delegation/credentialing requirements apply.
- **Setting / context:** Texas, including non-facility-based and facility-based practice settings.
- **Time horizon:** Current as of September 3, 2026, with later re-verification required when law/rules change.

## Decision boundaries

A proposition is considered sufficiently supported for the project when:

- the controlling statute or formally adopted rule supports it; or
- an official BON/TMB interpretation clearly explains how the controlling law/rule is applied and no contradictory higher authority is identified.

A proposition remains **unresolved** when:

- only secondary commentary or prior academic work supports it;
- the source is outdated or predates a material statutory/rule amendment;
- two official authorities appear inconsistent;
- the result depends on facts not yet defined, such as facility type, population focus, employer privileging, or the exact delegation mechanism.

## Scope

### Include

- APRN licensure and PMHNP role/population-focus limits.
- Professional vs individual scope of practice.
- Delegation of medical aspects of care.
- Prescriptive authority agreements.
- Facility-based protocols/other written authorization.
- Prescriptive authority number and renewal requirements.
- Physician delegation and TMB registration requirements.
- Controlled-substance prescribing, including Schedule II limitations.
- DEA, Texas PMP, electronic prescribing, and consultation requirements.
- Telemedicine prescribing and setting-specific limits.
- Employer credentialing/privileging as a distinct additional layer.

### Exclude

- Individualized legal advice.
- Contract interpretation for a specific employer.
- Malpractice coverage analysis.
- Billing/reimbursement except where it directly affects authority to provide a service.
- Advocacy about changing Texas law in the initial research sprint.
- Patient-specific treatment recommendations or PHI.

## Evidence priorities

- **Primary legal authorities:** Texas Occupations Code, Texas Health & Safety Code, Texas Administrative Code / formally adopted BON and TMB rules.
- **Official interpretive authorities:** Texas BON and TMB current webpages, FAQs, bulletins, and registration guidance.
- **Federal authorities:** DEA and other federal prescribing requirements when applicable.
- **Professional scope:** Current national PMHNP role/population-focus standards where needed to define professional scope.
- **Connected Drive sources to inspect first:** prior user-authored PMHNP coursework discussing Texas APRN scope; useful only for orientation and identifying citations to re-verify.

## Recency requirements

- **Search/review date:** September 3, 2026.
- **Legal/regulatory claims:** must be checked against current official sources before external publication or practice-policy reliance.
- **Older BON/TMB documents:** may explain history but should not override newer statutes, rules, or current official guidance.

## Safety / interpretation notes

- Legal scope, physician delegation, individual competence, and employer privileges are separate layers.
- Physician delegation cannot expand the PMHNP's licensed role/population-focus scope.
- A professional-scope conclusion does not automatically establish prescriptive authority.
- Controlled-substance authority must be analyzed separately from authority to prescribe non-controlled legend/dangerous drugs.
- This project summarizes regulatory research; it does not provide individualized legal advice.
