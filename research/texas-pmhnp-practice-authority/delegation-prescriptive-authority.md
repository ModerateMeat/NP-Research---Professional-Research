# Texas PMHNP Physician Delegation + Prescriptive Authority

**Slice:** 3 of Issue #7  
**Review date:** September 3, 2026  
**Status:** current working synthesis based on Texas BON/TMB/statutory sources

## Bottom line

Texas separates two related but different physician-delegation questions:

1. **Delegation for medical aspects of care** — the written delegation mechanism required for an APRN to provide medical aspects of care within the APRN's licensed role/population focus.
2. **Delegation of prescriptive authority** — the additional authority required when the APRN will order or prescribe drugs or medical devices.

A PMHNP who does not prescribe may still need a written physician delegation protocol if the position involves medical aspects of care such as formulating medical diagnoses, determining medical plans of care, ordering diagnostic studies, or making other medical determinations.

When prescribing is involved, the PMHNP must also have the applicable Texas prescriptive authority and physician-delegated prescribing mechanism.

## 1. General written delegation protocol / practice agreement

Current Texas BON guidance states that APRNs practicing in Texas must have a collaborative relationship and written delegation mechanism with a Texas-licensed physician for **medical aspects of patient care**.

Examples identified by BON include:

- creating a medical plan of care;
- ordering laboratory or other diagnostic tests;
- formulating medical diagnoses or determining the absence of a medical diagnosis;
- ordering/prescribing drugs or medical devices; and
- making certain medical determinations such as medical stability or fitness for duty.

Board Rule 221.13(d) is the key nursing-rule framework for this written authorization.

### Required characteristics of the general delegation mechanism

BON's Rule 221.13 materials state that protocols or other written authorization should be:

- jointly developed by the APRN and appropriate physician(s);
- signed by the APRN and physician(s);
- reviewed and re-signed at least annually;
- maintained in the APRN's practice setting; and
- available as necessary to verify authority to provide medical aspects of care.

The document does not need to micromanage every step for every disease, symptom, or condition. It should support professional judgment commensurate with the APRN's education and experience.

## 2. A delegation protocol is not automatically the same thing as a PAA

Texas BON's January 2026 guidance specifically distinguishes the general delegation protocol/practice agreement from the **prescriptive authority agreement (PAA)**.

- The **general delegation protocol** authorizes medical aspects of care under Board Rule 221.13(d).
- The **PAA** is the additional statutory/rule mechanism used to delegate prescribing/ordering authority in applicable practice settings under Board Rule 222.5 and Texas Occupations Code Chapter 157.

The two can be combined into one document when the parties choose and the combined document satisfies all applicable requirements.

### Practical rule

Do not assume that a document called a "collaborative agreement," "practice agreement," or "protocol" automatically satisfies the PAA requirements. Review the substance of the document against Rules 221.13, 222.5, 222.6, and Chapter 157 as applicable.

## 3. Prescribing requires a separate authority check

Before a Texas PMHNP orders or prescribes a drug or medical device, verify:

- active Texas APRN licensure in the applicable PMHNP role/population focus;
- a valid Texas prescription authorization/prescriptive authority number;
- physician-delegated prescriptive authority;
- a PAA or qualifying facility-based protocol/other written authorization, depending on the setting;
- that the drug/device category is authorized by the delegation document;
- any additional controlled-substance requirements; and
- employer credentialing/policy requirements.

Prescriptive authority does not expand the PMHNP's role/population-focus scope.

## 4. Non-facility-based practice: written PAA pathway

For a non-facility-based practice in which an APRN prescribes, current BON guidance identifies the written PAA as the applicable prescriptive-delegation mechanism.

Examples of locations that should **not** automatically be treated as qualifying facility-based practices include freestanding clinics, centers, and other medical practices merely associated with, owned by, or operated by a hospital or long-term-care facility.

A clinic's corporate relationship to a hospital is therefore not enough by itself to assume the facility-based protocol exception applies.

## 5. Minimum PAA content

Current BON guidance states that a PAA must be written and include, at minimum, the following categories:

1. names, addresses, and professional license numbers of the parties;
2. the nature of the practice and practice location(s)/setting(s);
3. the types/categories of drugs or devices that may be prescribed, or categories that may not be prescribed;
4. a general consultation/referral plan;
5. a plan for patient emergencies;
6. the general process for communication and sharing patient-care information;
7. a quality-assurance and improvement plan that includes chart review and required meetings;
8. alternate-physician information when alternate physician supervision/delegation will be used; and
9. dates/signatures of the parties.

The exact wording should be checked against the current statute/rule when drafting or reviewing an actual agreement.

## 6. Quality-assurance meetings

For PAAs subject to the current post-September 1, 2019 framework, BON guidance states that quality-assurance meetings must occur **at least monthly**.

Those monthly meetings do not have to be face-to-face and may occur through telecommunication or similar technology that permits meaningful dialogue.

BON identifies the required meeting subject matter as including:

- sharing information relating to patient treatment and care;
- needed changes in patient-care plans;
- referral issues; and
- discussion of patient-care improvement.

The parties may meet more frequently than monthly if their agreement requires it.

## 7. Chart review

Texas requires physician medical-record review as part of the PAA quality-assurance framework, but current BON guidance states that the law does **not** establish a universal fixed number or percentage of charts.

The parties determine the appropriate amount of chart review based on factors such as:

- APRN experience;
- length of the APRN/physician working relationship;
- whether they practice at the same site; and
- complexity of the patient population and care.

The agreement should make the chart-review plan clear enough that compliance can be demonstrated.

## 8. Annual review and retention

The PAA and amendments must be reviewed at least annually, dated, and signed by the parties.

Current BON/statutory guidance also requires the APRN to retain a copy of a terminated PAA until the **second anniversary of the termination date**.

The agreement must be made available to the appropriate licensing board when requested. BON guidance states that a requested PAA must be produced within **three business days** of the request.

## 9. TMB registration of prescriptive delegation

When prescriptive authority is delegated, Texas BON's January 2026 guidance identifies an additional Texas Medical Board registration requirement.

BON describes a two-step process:

1. the APRN completes the APRN portion of the TMB prescriptive-delegation registration; and
2. the Texas-licensed physician completes/certifies the physician portion in the TMB system.

A change to the delegating physician for prescriptive authority should therefore trigger review of the TMB registration relationship rather than simply changing an internal clinic document.

The Texas BON does not administer the TMB registration system.

## 10. Physician physical presence and countersignature

The physician is not required to be physically present at the APRN's practice location merely because medical acts have been delegated.

BON guidance also states that an APRN's order is not automatically required to be countersigned by the physician solely because the APRN is practicing under delegated authority.

This does **not** eliminate the physician-accessibility, consultation, QA, chart-review, or agreement requirements that otherwise apply.

## 11. Facility-based practice pathway

At a qualifying **hospital** or **licensed long-term-care facility** practice, an APRN with the required licensure and prescriptive authority may prescribe through:

- a PAA that satisfies Rule 222.5; or
- facility-based protocols/other written authorization developed in accordance with facility medical-staff policies under Rule 222.6.

### Hospital facility-based delegation

BON Rule 222.6 materials identify eligible delegating physicians in a hospital facility-based practice as including:

- the medical director;
- chief of medical staff;
- chair of the credentialing committee;
- department chair; or
- another physician who consents to a request by the medical director or chief of medical staff to delegate.

### Long-term-care facility-based delegation

For a licensed long-term-care facility-based practice, BON guidance identifies the medical director as the delegating physician for the facility-based protocol pathway.

### Facility protocol maintenance

Facility-based protocols/other written authorization must be agreed to/signed, reviewed at least annually, and maintained in the APRN's practice setting under the applicable rule/facility framework.

## 12. Prescriptive-delegation ratios and facility limits

Current BON guidance states:

- in ordinary non-exempt practice settings, one physician may delegate prescriptive authority to no more than **seven full-time-equivalent APRNs and PAs combined (1:7 FTE)**;
- facility-based hospital practices and practices serving medically underserved populations have statutory exceptions to that delegate-number limitation; and
- a physician using the **facility-based protocol** pathway may delegate at no more than **one licensed hospital** or **two long-term-care facilities** under the applicable framework.

These limits apply to prescriptive delegation and should not be generalized into a claim that every form of physician/APRN collaboration is governed by the same numeric cap.

## 13. More than one delegating physician

BON guidance states there is no fixed limit on the number of physicians who may delegate prescriptive authority to one APRN so long as the requirements for each delegation relationship are satisfied.

If alternates are used, the applicable PAA terms and QA responsibilities should clearly identify their role.

## 14. What this means for a Texas PMHNP employment/onboarding check

Before beginning PMHNP work involving medical diagnosis/management and/or prescribing, ask:

### General medical delegation

- [ ] Is there a written delegation protocol/practice agreement with a Texas-licensed physician for medical aspects of care?
- [ ] Is it signed, current, reviewed annually, and maintained at the practice site?
- [ ] Does it actually cover the medical acts expected in the position?

### Prescribing

- [ ] Does the PMHNP have active Texas prescriptive authority?
- [ ] Is prescribing delegated through a valid PAA or qualifying facility-based protocol?
- [ ] Does the document authorize the drug/device categories expected in practice?
- [ ] Have required chart-review and monthly QA-meeting processes been defined?
- [ ] Has the prescriptive-delegation relationship been registered with TMB?

### Setting

- [ ] Is the site truly a qualifying hospital/LTC facility-based practice, or is it a clinic/other non-facility site that requires the PAA pathway?
- [ ] Does employer credentialing/privileging match the delegation document and PMHNP scope?

### Controlled substances

- [ ] If controlled substances will be prescribed, stop and complete the separate Schedule/DEA/PMP/e-prescribing analysis in Slice 4 before assuming authority.
