# Texas PMHNP Telemedicine + Practice-Location Rules

**Slice:** 5 of Issue #7  
**Review date:** September 3, 2026  
**Status:** current working synthesis; federal controlled-substance telemedicine authority must be rechecked before or after December 31, 2026

## Bottom line

For Texas PMHNP telemedicine, four questions must be answered separately:

1. **Where is the patient physically located?** That location determines the nursing-practice jurisdiction.
2. **Is the PMHNP licensed and authorized to practice in that jurisdiction?** Texas APRN licensure is single-state.
3. **Would the care/prescription be lawful and within scope if the encounter were in person?** Telemedicine does not expand PMHNP scope or Texas prescriptive authority.
4. **If a controlled substance is involved, do both Texas and federal controlled-substance telemedicine rules permit it?** The more restrictive applicable requirement still controls.

## 1. Nursing practice occurs where the patient is located

Texas BON's April 2026 guidance states that nursing practice is considered to occur where the **patient is physically located**, not where the nurse is sitting.

### Patient in Texas

When the patient is located within Texas, the PMHNP must comply with the Texas Nursing Practice Act, BON rules, and required Texas APRN licensure and authority.

### Patient outside Texas

When the patient is physically located in another state, territory, or country, the nurse must comply with that jurisdiction's nursing/licensure requirements even if the PMHNP is physically located in Texas.

Texas BON does not grant authority to practice advanced-practice nursing in another jurisdiction.

## 2. APRN licensure is not made multistate by the RN compact

The Nurse Licensure Compact may provide an RN privilege when its requirements are met, but Texas BON states that **APRN licensure remains single-state licensure**.

A Texas PMHNP planning to treat a patient located in another state via telemedicine must therefore determine whether the other state requires that state's APRN/NP license, a telemedicine registration, or another authorization.

The fact that the PMHNP and patient are communicating electronically does not change this jurisdictional analysis.

## 3. Texas Rule 217.24 was amended effective April 9, 2026

Current 22 TAC §217.24 is titled **Telehealth Services and Telemedicine Medical Services in Nursing**.

The April 9, 2026 amendments implement statutory changes concerning informed-consent documentation and make the rule explicitly applicable to telehealth/telemedicine encounters, including audio-only formats where clinically appropriate.

### Same standard regardless of modality

Current Rule 217.24 states that the rule's standards apply whether the interaction is conducted by video or audio-only format.

Telemedicine does not permit a lower standard of assessment, diagnosis, treatment planning, prescribing, documentation, or professional judgment than would apply to an in-person encounter.

## 4. Informed consent under the current Texas rule

Current Rule 217.24 requires the nurse to document informed consent for telehealth or telemedicine services in the medical record.

Consent may be:

- written; or
- verbal.

If consent is verbal, the record must include the date the consent was given. If a responsible party provides consent, the record must identify that person's name and relationship to the patient.

### Retention

The current rule requires telehealth/telemedicine informed-consent records to be retained for at least **seven years from the date of last treatment by a nurse**, unless another law requires longer retention.

For a patient under age 18, the consent record must be retained until the patient reaches age 21 or for seven years from the date of last treatment, whichever is longer.

## 5. A telemedicine prescription must meet the in-person validity standard

Rule 217.24 states that the validity of a prescription issued through telemedicine is determined by the **same standards that would apply in an in-person setting**.

The prescription must:

- be for a legitimate medical purpose;
- arise from the required patient-practitioner relationship under Texas law; and
- comply with all other applicable federal and state prescription laws.

### Critical implication

Telemedicine is a **modality**, not a new source of prescribing authority.

A Texas PMHNP cannot use telemedicine to prescribe a drug, device, or schedule that the PMHNP would not otherwise be legally authorized to prescribe under Texas scope, delegation, PAA/facility protocol, Schedule II, DEA, or other rules.

## 6. Texas controlled-substance rules still apply in telemedicine

Current Texas BON guidance states that all limitations in Rule 222.8 continue to apply when controlled substances are prescribed through telemedicine.

This includes, as applicable:

- Texas prescriptive authority;
- physician-delegated prescriptive authority;
- PAA/facility-based authorization;
- Schedule III-V 90-day/physician-consultation limits;
- Texas's narrow Schedule II delegation circumstances;
- the APRN's DEA registration;
- PMP requirements;
- electronic-prescribing rules; and
- other patient/setting-specific limitations.

### Texas PMP

The same mandatory PMP checks that apply in person apply in telemedicine. The modality does not create an exception to the PMP requirement.

## 7. Federal controlled-substance telemedicine flexibilities through December 31, 2026

DEA and HHS issued the **Fourth Temporary Extension of COVID-19 Telemedicine Flexibilities for Prescription of Controlled Medications**, effective January 1 through December 31, 2026.

Under the federal temporary extension, a DEA-registered practitioner may, when all federal conditions are met:

- prescribe Schedule II-V controlled medications through an audio-video telemedicine encounter without first conducting an in-person medical evaluation; and
- prescribe qualifying Schedule III-V narcotic controlled medications approved for maintenance or withdrawal-management treatment of opioid use disorder through qualifying audio-only telemedicine encounters.

These federal flexibilities remain subject to **applicable state law**.

### Texas is still controlling when Texas is more restrictive

The federal temporary rule does **not** grant a Texas PMHNP new state authority.

For example, even though the federal temporary extension may permit a DEA-registered practitioner to prescribe Schedule II controlled medication through qualifying audio-video telemedicine without a prior in-person examination, Texas still restricts physician delegation of Schedule II authority to the narrow hospital facility-based and hospice circumstances described in the Texas controlled-substance slice.

Therefore, a Texas PMHNP cannot rely on the federal telemedicine extension to create routine outpatient Schedule II authority.

## 8. Federal rules are time-sensitive and must be rechecked after 2026

The fourth federal temporary extension expires **December 31, 2026**, unless federal law/rules change again.

DEA/HHS also finalized separate rules concerning:

- expansion of buprenorphine treatment through telemedicine; and
- continuity of care for qualifying Veterans Affairs patients.

DEA has explained that practitioners who fall within one of those final rules may, while the fourth temporary extension is in force, continue using the broader temporary-flexibility pathway when its requirements are met.

A proposed federal special-registration framework for telemedicine has also been under rulemaking consideration. Do not treat a proposed special-registration framework as final authority unless a final rule has actually become effective.

### Harness rule

**Any repo statement about federal telemedicine prescribing after December 31, 2026 must be re-researched before use.**

Do not copy this 2026 federal framework forward into 2027 material without verification.

## 9. Chronic pain with scheduled drugs under Texas Rule 217.24

Current Texas Rule 217.24 contains specific limitations for chronic-pain treatment with scheduled drugs through telemedicine.

The rule restricts use of telemedicine for chronic pain with scheduled drugs by means other than audio-video two-way communication unless the patient meets the rule's established-patient/continuation conditions, including that:

- the patient is an established patient of the APRN being treated for chronic pain;
- the prescription is identical to the prescription issued at the previous visit; and
- the patient has been seen within the previous 90 days by the prescribing APRN, physician, or other qualifying health professional, either in person or by audio-video two-way telemedicine.

When the chronic-pain exception is used, the record must document the exception and why a telemedicine visit was conducted rather than an in-person visit.

This pain-specific section should not be generalized to all psychiatric controlled-substance prescribing.

## 10. Acute pain

Current Rule 217.24 states that telemedicine may be used for treatment of acute pain with scheduled drugs unless otherwise prohibited by federal or state law.

That statement does not override Texas's separate acute-pain opioid supply restrictions, Rule 222.8 limitations, DEA requirements, or other applicable prescribing standards.

## 11. Audio-only care

Current Texas Rule 217.24 recognizes audio-only telehealth/telemedicine encounters and applies the same professional standards to them, but the fact that audio-only interaction is recognized does not mean every clinical service or every controlled-substance prescription is appropriate or lawful by audio-only care.

The PMHNP must still determine whether:

- the clinical assessment can meet the standard of care;
- Texas prescribing rules permit the medication and circumstance;
- federal controlled-substance telemedicine rules permit audio-only prescribing for that medication/use; and
- employer/platform policy permits the modality.

As of the September 3, 2026 federal framework, the broad temporary flexibility for Schedule II-V prescribing without prior in-person evaluation uses **audio-video** telemedicine, while a narrower audio-only federal pathway applies to qualifying Schedule III-V narcotic medications approved for opioid-use-disorder maintenance/withdrawal treatment.

## 12. Telemedicine onboarding checklist for a Texas PMHNP

Before seeing a patient remotely, verify:

### Jurisdiction

- [ ] Confirm the patient's physical location at the time of the encounter.
- [ ] Confirm the PMHNP has the APRN/telemedicine authority required by that jurisdiction.
- [ ] Do not rely on the RN Nurse Licensure Compact as though it were an APRN multistate license.

### Texas practice

- [ ] If the patient is in Texas, verify active Texas APRN/PMHNP licensure and scope.
- [ ] Confirm general physician-delegation and PAA/facility authorization requirements are satisfied when applicable.
- [ ] Document telehealth/telemedicine informed consent under current Rule 217.24.
- [ ] Meet the same standard of care and prescribing validity standards that apply in person.

### Controlled substances

- [ ] Verify that the medication's Texas schedule-specific authority exists before considering federal telemedicine flexibility.
- [ ] Confirm DEA registration and applicable federal authority.
- [ ] Complete Texas PMP requirements when triggered.
- [ ] Follow electronic-prescribing requirements.
- [ ] If chronic pain/scheduled drugs are involved, apply the specific Rule 217.24 limitations.
- [ ] Recheck the federal telemedicine rule before relying on it after December 31, 2026.

### Employer/platform

- [ ] Confirm credentialing/privileging and malpractice coverage for telemedicine.
- [ ] Confirm the technology supports privacy, documentation, identity/location verification, emergency response, and any required audio-video capability.
- [ ] Have a plan for emergencies, patient deterioration, involuntary-treatment concerns, or need for in-person assessment in the patient's location.
