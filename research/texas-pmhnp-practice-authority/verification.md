## Claim families verified

### 1. Texas APRN/PMHNP licensure

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Texas APRN licensure is role/population-focus specific rather than a generic license to practice in every APRN specialty.
- National certification is a licensure requirement for modern APRN graduates but is not itself the Texas APRN license.
- A valid RN privilege to practice in Texas is the base licensure layer.
- The RN/LVN Nurse Licensure Compact does not create multistate Texas APRN authority.
- BON application guidance provides a recent-graduate pathway in which qualifying APRN program completion within the previous 24 months can satisfy the current-practice/CNE recency alternatives for initial application eligibility.
- Current BON application guidance requires at least 500 separate, non-duplicated clinical hours in each role/population focus sought for programs completed on/after January 1, 2003.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas BON APRN application materials, BON scope/FAQ guidance, recognized-certification list, Texas Occupations Code Chapter 301.

**Freshness trigger:** recheck BON application and recognized-certification requirements before relying on them for a future application or publication.

---

### 2. Professional scope vs individual scope vs employer privileges

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Formal APRN education/licensure in the PMHNP role/population focus defines the foundational professional lane.
- Individual scope is further limited by education, training, competence, patient needs, current standards, and circumstances.
- Additional post-licensure training may deepen practice within the existing role/population focus but does not substitute for formal APRN education/licensure in another role/population focus.
- Employer credentialing/privileging may be narrower than broad professional/legal scope.
- Physician delegation cannot expand the APRN into a role/population focus the APRN does not hold.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas BON APRN Scope of Practice and APRN Practice FAQ.

---

### 3. General physician delegation vs prescriptive delegation

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Texas distinguishes the written physician delegation mechanism for medical aspects of APRN care from the additional mechanism used to delegate prescriptive authority.
- Current BON guidance states that the written medical-delegation protocol/practice agreement is relevant even when the APRN is not prescribing if medical aspects of care are being provided.
- A PAA is used for applicable non-facility-based prescriptive delegation.
- Qualifying hospital/LTC facility-based practices may use the applicable facility-based protocol/written-authorization pathway.
- General delegation and PAA requirements may be combined into one document only when the combined document satisfies all applicable requirements.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas BON January 2026 Bulletin, Rules 221.13/222.5/222.6, Texas Occupations Code Chapter 157, BON FAQ.

---

### 4. PAA quality assurance and administration

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Current PAA guidance includes at least monthly quality-assurance meetings.
- Physician chart review is part of the quality-assurance framework; Texas does not impose one universal chart-review percentage for every PAA.
- PAAs are reviewed/signed at least annually.
- A terminated PAA is retained through the second anniversary of termination.
- Prescriptive delegation is registered through the Texas Medical Board process.
- A freestanding clinic is not automatically converted into a statutory facility-based practice merely because it is affiliated with or owned by a hospital.

**Freshness trigger:** recheck if the delegating physician, practice entity, location, or facility classification changes.

---

### 5. Schedule III-V controlled substances

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Texas may permit physician delegation of Schedule III-V prescribing to an APRN when all scope, prescriptive-authority, delegation, DEA, and other requirements are satisfied.
- Board Rule 222.8 establishes the APRN 90-day supply/continued-therapy physician-consultation framework.
- Current BON guidance interprets the consultation requirement based on the patient's ongoing controlled-substance therapy rather than resetting merely because the patient is new to the APRN.
- Additional physician consultation/documentation requirements apply to Schedule III-V prescribing for a child younger than two years.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas BON Rule 222.8 and July 2025 BON guidance.

---

### 6. Schedule II controlled substances

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Texas Schedule II physician delegation to APRNs is substantially narrower than Schedule III-V authority.
- Current Texas authority limits APRN Schedule II ordering/prescribing to defined qualifying hospital facility-based patient circumstances and qualified hospice care.
- A freestanding emergency department affiliated with a hospital does not qualify as the hospital site for this Schedule II delegation pathway under current BON guidance.
- Current BON FAQ guidance limits an APRN Schedule II hospital-discharge prescription to the qualifying facility-based pharmacy pathway.
- Routine outpatient psychiatric use of a Schedule II medication does not itself create Texas PMHNP Schedule II authority.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas Occupations Code Chapter 157, BON Rule 222.8, BON FAQ, July 2025 BON controlled-substance guidance.

---

### 7. DEA authority

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- The APRN must have the applicable state authority before DEA registration can support controlled-substance prescribing.
- DEA authority does not enlarge Texas schedule authority.
- The PMHNP uses the PMHNP's own DEA registration rather than prescribing under the delegating physician's DEA registration.
- Texas no longer requires the former DPS controlled-substance registration.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas BON DEA guidance and current DEA registration guidance.

**Freshness trigger:** recheck DEA registration/address/state requirements when changing states or principal practice locations.

---

### 8. Texas PMP

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Texas requires a PMP check before prescribing opioids, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, or carisoprodol, subject to statutory exceptions.
- Health and Safety Code §481.0765 includes cancer, sickle cell disease, and hospice care among the statutory exceptions when the required notation is made.
- Current BON guidance requires documentation of PMP review and the rationale for the controlled-substance prescription.
- Authorized staff/agents may perform a query where permitted, but the APRN remains responsible for review and prescribing documentation.

### Version-drift resolution

Some older BON Rule 228.2 text lists cancer/hospice without the later statutory sickle-cell exception. The project follows the **controlling current statute** and current BON FAQ for the sickle-cell exception rather than silently treating older rule-compilation wording as superior to statute.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas Health and Safety Code §§481.0764-.0765, Texas State Board of Pharmacy PMP materials, current BON FAQ.

---

### 9. Electronic controlled-substance prescribing

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Texas generally requires controlled-substance prescriptions to be electronic.
- Health and Safety Code §481.0755 contains statutory circumstances in which an electronic prescription is not required.
- An individual prescriber may qualify for a licensing-agency waiver under the applicable framework.
- A waiver held by a delegating physician does not automatically extend to the APRN.

**Primary/current sources used:** Texas Health and Safety Code §§481.0755-.0756 and BON e-prescribing guidance.

---

### 10. Acute-pain opioid rule

**Verified baseline conclusion:**

The separate Texas acute-pain opioid rule generally limits an opioid prescription to a 10-day supply with no refill, subject to the statute's defined exclusions/exceptions. This is a separate rule from the APRN's 90-day Schedule III-V controlled-substance framework and must not be conflated with it.

**Primary source:** Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 481.

---

### 11. Texas telehealth/telemedicine jurisdiction

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- Nursing practice occurs where the patient is physically located.
- A patient located in Texas triggers Texas nursing/APRN authority; a patient located elsewhere triggers that jurisdiction's requirements.
- Texas APRN licensure remains single-state.
- Telemedicine does not expand PMHNP scope, delegation, or prescriptive authority.

**Primary/current source:** Texas BON April 2026 Bulletin.

---

### 12. Texas Rule 217.24 — current version

**Verified baseline conclusions:**

- The amended Rule 217.24 became effective April 9, 2026.
- The current rule applies telehealth/telemedicine standards across video and audio-only formats.
- Informed consent may be written or verbal, with required documentation.
- Consent-retention requirements are at least seven years after the last treatment by a nurse, with the longer minor-patient rule specified by the regulation.
- Telemedicine prescriptions must satisfy the same validity/standard-of-care requirements that apply in person.
- Federal/Texas controlled-substance restrictions continue to apply.

### Version-drift resolution

Older BON rule compilations contain the pre-April-2026 version of Rule 217.24. The project uses the **April 3, 2026 Texas Register adoption / April 9, 2026 effective rule** as the controlling current version for the baseline.

---

### 13. Federal controlled-substance telemedicine — 2026 temporary rule

**Verified baseline conclusions as of September 3, 2026:**

- DEA/HHS's Fourth Temporary Extension is effective January 1 through December 31, 2026.
- Under the temporary federal pathway, qualifying DEA-registered practitioners may prescribe Schedule II-V controlled medications through audio-video telemedicine without a prior in-person medical evaluation when all other requirements are met.
- A narrower audio-only pathway applies to qualifying Schedule III-V narcotic medications approved for opioid-use-disorder maintenance/withdrawal management.
- The federal flexibility remains subject to state law and therefore does not override Texas's narrower PMHNP Schedule II delegation limits.
- DEA/HHS also have separate final rules involving buprenorphine telemedicine and VA continuity of care; DEA states practitioners covered by those rules may continue to use the broader fourth-extension pathway while it remains effective when its requirements are met.
- The special-registration framework announced during federal rulemaking should not be treated as final operative authority merely because a proposed rule exists.
