**Jurisdiction:** Texas  
**Baseline review date:** September 3, 2026  
**Parent issue:** #7

## Purpose

This project is a current, source-grounded reference for the legal and professional boundaries that govern psychiatric-mental health nurse practitioner practice in Texas.

It supports professional research, onboarding/employment questions, policy interpretation, and future writing. It is **not individualized legal advice** and should not replace consultation with the Texas Board of Nursing (BON), Texas Medical Board (TMB), an employer's credentialing/legal team, or qualified counsel when a specific legal question is material.

## Source hierarchy

When sources conflict or differ in authority, use this order:

1. Texas statutes and formally adopted administrative rules.
2. Current Texas BON and TMB official guidance interpreting those authorities.
3. Federal authorities when federal law governs the issue (for example DEA requirements).
4. Professional specialty standards for the PMHNP role/population focus.
5. Employer credentialing, privileging, policies, and contracts for organization-specific limits.
6. Prior academic work or secondary commentary only as orientation—not as controlling authority.

## Core distinction

Three questions must remain separate:

1. **Legal/professional scope:** Is the activity within the PMHNP role/population focus and Texas law/rules?
2. **Delegated authority:** If the activity involves delegated medical acts or prescribing, is the required physician delegation mechanism in place?
3. **Individual/organizational authority:** Is the PMHNP competent, credentialed, privileged, and permitted by the employer to perform the activity?

A "yes" to one does not automatically create a "yes" to the others.

## How to use this project

For a practical employment/onboarding question, start with `pre-practice-checklist.md` and follow its links into the detailed files when an item is uncertain.

For research or publication, start with `sources.md` and `authority-map.md`, then recheck any time-sensitive proposition against the current controlling source before external use.

## Mandatory freshness triggers

Re-research the affected claim before use when:

- a Texas statute, BON/TMB rule, or official guidance has changed;
- a new Texas legislative session materially affects APRN practice;
- BON changes recognized PMHNP certification requirements;
- the practice setting or delegating physician changes;
- a federal DEA controlled-substance/telemedicine rule changes; or
- any controlled-substance telemedicine claim will be used after **December 31, 2026**.

## Update rule

A publication-facing statement about current law, regulation, licensure, prescribing, or telemedicine must be checked against the current official authority if the baseline review is no longer reasonably current or if a known change trigger has occurred. Do not copy a dated conclusion forward merely because it already exists in the repo.
