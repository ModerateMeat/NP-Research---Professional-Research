# Texas PMHNP Licensure + Role/Population-Focus Boundaries

**Slice:** 2 of Issue #7  
**Review date:** September 3, 2026  
**Status:** current working synthesis based on Texas BON official sources; recheck before publication if rules change

## Bottom line

Texas does not issue a generic APRN license that authorizes practice in every advanced-practice specialty. APRN licensure is tied to the **advanced-practice role and population focus in which the nurse was formally educated, nationally certified, and licensed by the Texas Board of Nursing (BON)**.

For a PMHNP, the practical sequence is:

**valid Texas RN practice privilege → qualifying APRN education → congruent national PMHNP certification → Texas APRN licensure in the PMHNP role/population focus → individual scope/competence → physician delegation when required for medical aspects of care → employer credentialing/privileging.**

Each layer matters. Completing one layer does not automatically satisfy the others.

## 1. A valid RN privilege is the base license layer

The BON requires an applicant for Texas APRN licensure to hold a valid privilege to practice as an RN in Texas.

That RN privilege may be based on:

- a current Texas RN license; or
- a valid multistate RN license/privilege from the nurse's primary state of residence when the applicable Nurse Licensure Compact requirements are met.

The RN compact privilege is a **base RN privilege**. It does not turn the APRN license itself into a multistate APRN license.

Current BON guidance states that APRN licensure remains single-state licensure and the RN/LVN Nurse Licensure Compact does not itself extend Texas APRN authority into another state.

## 2. Texas APRN licensure is role/population-focus specific

The BON does not license a nurse simply as a generic "APRN" for unrestricted advanced practice.

The license is granted for a specific APRN role and population focus that is congruent with the nurse's formal APRN educational preparation.

For this project, the relevant combination is the **nurse practitioner role with the psychiatric/mental-health population focus**.

### Consequence

A PMHNP cannot legally acquire the full scope of another APRN role or population focus merely through:

- employer orientation;
- physician delegation;
- on-the-job experience;
- continuing education;
- a short procedural course; or
- a specialty certification that is not itself the required role/population-focus APRN education and certification.

To add another APRN role/population focus, BON guidance requires the additional formal APRN education, congruent national certification, and separate Texas licensure/application for that role/population focus.

## 3. National certification is necessary but is not the Texas APRN license

For modern APRN graduates, national certification in the role/population focus is a Texas licensure requirement.

BON guidance is explicit that being nationally certified does **not** by itself authorize a person to practice or hold themselves out as an APRN in Texas. The nurse must also obtain Texas BON APRN licensure.

Likewise, a new graduate may not begin practicing as an APRN merely because the graduate has completed the APRN program. BON states that new graduates must provide evidence of current national certification before becoming eligible for advanced-practice licensure/approval.

## 4. PMHNP certifications currently recognized by Texas BON

The BON's January 2025 recognized-certification list identifies the following certifications for the Psychiatric/Mental Health Nurse Practitioner role/population focus:

- American Nurses Credentialing Center (ANCC) — PMHNP (across the lifespan);
- American Academy of Nurse Practitioners Certification Board (AANPCB) — PMHNP; and
- legacy ANCC Adult PMHNP certification for individuals who already hold/renew that discontinued initial-exam credential.

This list should be rechecked if a certification pathway changes.

**Official source:** https://www.bon.texas.gov/pdfs/aprn_pdfs/APRN-Certification-Exams.pdf

## 5. Education must be congruent with the licensure sought

The BON APRN application materials require completion of an advanced-practice nursing educational program accredited by an organization recognized by the Board and compliant with Rule 221.3.

For applicants whose APRN program was completed on or after January 1, 2003, BON application guidance states that the program must include at least **500 separate, non-duplicated clinical hours** in each advanced-practice role and population focus for which licensure is sought.

The BON's APRN education framework also treats advanced health assessment, advanced physiology/pathophysiology, and advanced pharmacology/pharmacotherapeutics as foundational graduate-level content supporting the role/population-focus curriculum and supervised direct patient-care experiences.

The educational record must support the actual role/population focus requested. A broad graduate nursing degree alone is not enough if it does not meet the APRN educational requirements for the role/population focus.

## 6. Recent graduates have an important recency pathway

Current BON APRN application guidance requires either:

- at least **400 hours of practice** in the advanced-practice role/population focus within the previous 24 calendar months **or** completion of the APRN educational program within the previous 24 calendar months; and
- at least **20 contact hours of continuing nursing education** in the advanced role/population focus within the previous 24 calendar months **or** completion of the APRN educational program within the previous 24 calendar months.

This means the BON has a specific pathway for recent graduates who have not yet accumulated post-graduation APRN practice hours.

These are **licensure eligibility recency requirements**, not a statement that a graduate may practice as an APRN before national certification and BON APRN licensure are issued.

## 7. APRN titles are protected

BON guidance states that APRN titles/designations may not be used in a way that implies Texas APRN licensure before the license is actually issued.

National certification credentials that imply APRN status should not be presented to the public as though they establish Texas APRN licensure when the Texas APRN license has not yet been granted.

Once licensed, the APRN must identify the APRN designation and the licensure title granted by BON when providing patient care, subject to the applicable BON rules.

## 8. Professional scope and individual scope are different

A Texas PMHNP has both:

### Professional scope

The broad functions recognized for the PMHNP role/population focus by professional organizations, APRN education/competency standards, and Texas law/rules.

### Individual scope

The subset the individual PMHNP is actually prepared and competent to perform based on:

- graduate APRN education;
- post-licensure education/training that remains within the existing role/population focus;
- demonstrated competence;
- current evidence and standard of care;
- access to resources needed to manage complications;
- credentialing/privileging;
- employer policy; and
- the specific patient/setting circumstances.

An activity can fall within the broad PMHNP professional scope but still fall outside a particular PMHNP's current individual scope.

## 9. Additional training can deepen—but not replace—the licensed role/population focus

BON's current scope guidance recognizes that APRNs may add procedures or patient-care activities over their careers through appropriate education, training, and demonstrated competency **when those activities remain within the APRN's existing role/population focus**.

There are finite limits to this process. Additional continuing education or on-the-job training cannot substitute for a formal APRN program and BON licensure in a different role or population focus.

### Working rule

**Training may deepen practice inside the PMHNP lane; it does not create a new APRN lane.**

## 10. Physician delegation cannot cure a scope problem

A physician's willingness to delegate a medical act does not make the act lawful for an APRN who is not educationally and legally prepared to perform it in the APRN's licensed role/population focus.

The PMHNP must first be within professional and individual APRN scope. Physician delegation is then analyzed as a separate requirement for delegated medical aspects of care.

## 11. Employer credentialing can be narrower than BON scope

Even when an activity is within the broad Texas PMHNP professional scope, an employer or facility may require:

- additional documented competency;
- credentialing;
- clinical privileges;
- supervision/orientation;
- internal protocols; or
- restrictions based on the patient population or service line.

Therefore, "within Texas PMHNP scope" does not automatically mean "authorized at this employer."

## 12. Working licensure checklist

Before a nurse begins practicing in Texas in the PMHNP role, verify:

- [ ] Valid RN privilege to practice in Texas.
- [ ] APRN educational program meets Texas Rule 221.3 requirements.
- [ ] Required direct clinical hours are documented.
- [ ] Current national certification is congruent with PMHNP education and recognized by BON.
- [ ] Texas BON APRN license in the PMHNP role/population focus has actually been issued.
- [ ] Required APRN designation/licensure title is used appropriately.
- [ ] The intended service is within PMHNP professional scope.
- [ ] The individual PMHNP has education/training and demonstrated competency for the service.
- [ ] Required physician delegation mechanisms are in place for applicable medical aspects of care.
- [ ] Employer credentialing/privileging/policies permit the activity.
- [ ] Prescriptive authority requirements are separately satisfied if prescribing is involved.
